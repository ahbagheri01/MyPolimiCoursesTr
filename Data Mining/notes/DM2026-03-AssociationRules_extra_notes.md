## Origin and Nature of Association Rule Mining

Association rule mining originated from the database systems community (IBM Almaden Research Center) rather than statistics or machine learning, driven by the desire to exploit 'market basket' transaction data previously discarded due to storage costs.

## Cross-Selling and Misleading Highly Frequent Rules

**Reference:** DM2026-2027-04-AssociationRules.pdf: slides 7, 12, 59

High confidence (even 100%) can produce misleading or uninteresting rules if the consequent item is universally frequent across most transactions (e.g. milk), demonstrating why raw confidence alone is insufficient and motivating measures like lift.

## Database Representations: Horizontal vs. Vertical (TID-lists)

**Reference:** DM2026-2027-04-AssociationRules.pdf: slides 9, 34

In the vertical database representation (tid-lists), support counting simplifies to fast set intersection between tid-lists rather than scanning transactions, transforming disk-bound database queries into in-memory set intersections.

## Decomposition into Frequent Itemset Mining and Rule Generation

**Reference:** DM2026-2027-04-AssociationRules.pdf: slides 13-14

In association rule mining, approximately 99.9% of the computational work is dedicated to frequent itemset mining, because rule generation from a discovered frequent itemset is straightforward and reuses the itemset support.

## Candidate Generation using Lexicographical Prefix Joining

**Reference:** DM2026-2027-04-AssociationRules.pdf: slides 27, 30-31

To prevent redundant combinations and unpruned infrequent branches when extending prefix trees, candidate itemsets are generated by ordering items lexicographically and joining siblings that share a common prefix (e.g. length k-2 for candidate k-itemsets).

## FP-Tree Construction and Reordering Strategy

**Reference:** DM2026-2027-04-AssociationRules.pdf: slide 42

Items within each transaction are sorted descendingly by global item frequency (with a lexicographical tiebreaker) to maximize prefix sharing near the root, ensuring the FP-tree remains compact.

## Itemsets vs Sequences: Commutativity and Ordering

**Reference:** DM2026-2027-04-AssociationRules.pdf: slides 72, 80

While itemset mining treats sets as unordered and commutative (AB equals BA), sequential pattern mining strictly distinguishes item arrival order (A followed by B differs from B followed by A), dramatically expanding candidate search spaces.
