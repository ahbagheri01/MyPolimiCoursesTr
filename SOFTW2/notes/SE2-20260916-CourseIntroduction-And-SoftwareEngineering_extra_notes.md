Final scores are rounded up to the higher integer, with the specific exception of 30.5, which is rounded down to 30.

## Written Exam 1 (WE1) Topics

**Reference:** 00.CourseIntro.pdf, slide 17

Written Exam 1 always contains an Alloy specification exercise, accompanied by two other questions typically drawn from testing, architectural availability assessment, or project management.

## Project Delivery and Evolution of Artifacts

**Reference:** 00.CourseIntro.pdf, slide 13

Updating and resubmitting intermediate deliverables (e.g. RASD with DD) is specifically aimed at maintaining cross-artifact consistency as project understanding evolves.

## Use of AI / LLMs and Oral Defense

AI/LLM tools are permitted for producing project artifacts, but the oral defense emphasizes evaluating whether students genuinely understand, control, and can defend design choices rather than relying passively on LLM outputs.

## Acceptance Testing & Peer Review Mechanism

**Reference:** 00.CourseIntro.pdf, slide 10

In the peer acceptance testing phase, evaluating group B on group A's code is not used to grade group A; instead, it assesses group B's ability to critically analyze software produced by others.

## CrowdStrike Incident & Software Malleability

**Reference:** 01.IntroSE.pdf, slide 3, 7

The CrowdStrike incident highlights the dangers of software malleability: developers made rapid changes without agreeing on a component interface, leading to an out-of-bounds error that cascaded into critical system failure.

## Modern Software Lifecycle vs. Traditional Waterfall

**Reference:** 01.IntroSE.pdf, slides 16, 21-22

A central motivation for modern flexible lifecycles over waterfall is avoiding service shutdowns by evolving 24/7 runtime services on the fly while running continuously.

## Modern Definition of Software Engineering: Multi-Agent Systems

**Reference:** 01.IntroSE.pdf, slide 4

The classical definition of software engineering as 'multi-person construction of multi-version software' is updated to 'multi-agent construction', acknowledging that software is now co-developed by human and AI agents working in collaboration.
